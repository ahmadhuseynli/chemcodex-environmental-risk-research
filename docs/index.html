Independent environmental-risk research

From a chemical SDS to a transparent environmental risk assessment.

A Safety Data Sheet can tell you a great deal about a chemical, but it cannot by itself answer whether a particular discharge will create unacceptable environmental risk. ChemCodex grew from trying to connect the missing pieces: chemical hazard, environmental exposure, effect thresholds and the decision to refine or escalate an assessment.

The project has gone through spreadsheets, mixture logic, SDS parsers, web prototypes, PEC/PNEC calculations and AI-assisted extraction experiments. The current direction is deliberately simpler: use AI where it helps organise evidence, keep the scientific rules explicit, and never hide scenario assumptions inside one unexplained score.

The problem the project is trying to solve

Environmental chemical review often lives across separate SDS files, regulatory lists, spreadsheets, discharge assumptions and specialist modelling. The useful part is not another hazard score. It is a traceable chain from evidence to decision.

1

What can the chemical do?

Aquatic toxicity, persistence, bioaccumulation, H-codes, composition and regulatory context.

2

What concentration will occur?

Release mass, duration, receiving environment, dilution, fate and the relevant environmental compartment.

3

Does exposure exceed effect?

Use a defensible effect threshold and compare it with exposure, while retaining uncertainty and the assumptions that created the result.

Hazard and risk are not interchangeable. A hazardous substance can have low scenario-specific exposure. A low-hazard material can still deserve higher-tier modelling when the release is large or the receiving environment is poorly mixed.

Current architecture

The recovered project contains many prototype generations. The most defensible architecture separates them into stages that can be tested independently.

Identity & evidence
product/components, source section, endpoints, reliability
Regulatory & hazard
versioned routing, aquatic hazard, PBT, metals, mixtures
Effect & exposure
PNEC derivation, mass release, water/sediment PEC
Risk & audit
RQ/RCR, uncertainty, escalation, rule/version record

The consolidated architecture now runs from identity and evidence extraction through regulatory routing, intrinsic hazard, effect assessment, exposure, risk characterisation, escalation and reporting/audit.

The design rule remains straightforward: AI may extract; deterministic rules decide. The extraction model should be replaceable without changing the scientific calculation rules.

What was actually built

This was not one application written once. Different prototypes tested different parts of the problem.

Excel / VBA hazard engine

Aquatic hazard statements, toxicity, PBT, bioaccumulation, regulatory lookup, mixture logic and summary macros.

SDS parsing

Rule-based extraction of components, H400-H413 statements and aquatic endpoints such as LC50, EC50, NOEC, LL50 and EL50.

Web prototypes

Streamlit and Flask branches moved the work from analyst spreadsheets toward an application and report-generation workflow.

JavaScript rules engine

Later browser logic added endpoint reliability, product/component evidence and more explicit mixture handling.

PEC / PNEC risk workbook

The project eventually separated source/exposure information from effect thresholds and calculated scenario-specific screening risk.

AI-assisted extraction

A later branch used an LLM to structure SDS evidence. The public architecture keeps that extraction layer separate from the risk rules.

The repository includes a deliberately limited parser and screening calculator. The calculator has no hidden default dilution or assessment factor: those have to be supplied as explicit scenario inputs.

What the technical audit clarified

The recovered workbooks and code contain useful scientific structure, but they also contain prototype assumptions that need to stay visible rather than becoming hidden defaults.

Bioaccumulation

Historical BCF/logKow screening was separated from formal REACH Annex XIII criteria. The public audit now records BCF >2,000 for B and >5,000 for vB; logKow remains supporting evidence rather than a final B/vB decision.

Mixtures and M-factors

Generic species weights and generic M-factor tables are retained as historical experiments, not universal rules. M-factors are substance-specific and tied to aquatic classification.

PEC and PNEC

Historical dilution factors, fixed assessment factors and the experimental default PEC are documented as prototype assumptions. Future values must come from the actual evidence set and release scenario.

Sediment and escalation

Sediment risk remains a higher-tier module requiring defensible partitioning, mixing-depth, density and bioavailability assumptions. Complex exposure should be handed to hydrodynamic or specialist assessment.

What survived the research

Hazard, exposure and risk need separate modules. Missing data should stay visible. Evidence quality matters. A regulatory-list match is a routing signal, not a universal proof of no risk. Simple screening needs an escalation path when physical exposure cannot be represented honestly.

What did not survive

Several older formulas were too simple: generic persistence conversions, broad mixture triggers, fixed PNEC factors, universal dilution assumptions and a failed one-class ML experiment. They are preserved as development history, not current scientific rules.

Where it can go next

The next serious step is not more interface work. It is a validated evidence and calculation engine.

Reviewed benchmark library

Manually checked SDS/component cases with locked sources and known expected outputs.

Separate extraction validation

Measure endpoint recall, unit accuracy, component linking and hallucination rate before judging the risk engine.

Rule-by-rule scientific QA

Every deterministic rule gets a named source, version, applicability statement, unit tests and edge cases.

Independent ERA comparison

Compare the screening engine with professionally completed environmental risk assessments, including cases that require refinement.

Higher-tier handoff

Route difficult cases to hydrodynamic modelling, whole-effluent testing, specialist metal assessment or monitoring.

Auditable environmental workflow

If validated, the same structure could support chemical inventory review, discharge screening, permitting and environmental-compliance QA.
